Blueberries
Vaccinium spp.
The blue fruit that gathers summer into sweetness.

NATURAL HISTORY
Blueberries belong to the Vaccinium family, a group of shrubs known for their bell-shaped flowers and dusky blue fruits. They thrive best in acidic soils and bright, open places, whether in woodland clearings, moorland kinlands or cultivated gardens. Their fruit carries both freshness and depth, ripening slowly into summer sweetness.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
Blueberries feed bees when in flower and later nourish birds, mammals and people with their abundant berries. Their shrubs offer cover close to the ground, and their fruiting ties together pollinators, foragers and seed-dispersers. They are a reminder that sweetness is part of ecology, not apart from it.

TRADITIONS & LORE
Blue fruits have long belonged to summer gathering, preserving and sharing. Whether eaten fresh, baked, dried or made into jam, blueberries carry a sense of abundance held in small form. They belong to the basket, the path and the berry-stained hand — gifts of season, memory and delight.
