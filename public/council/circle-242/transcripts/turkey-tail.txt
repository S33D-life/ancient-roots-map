Turkey Tail
Trametes versicolor
The layered fan that returns wood to earth.

NATURAL HISTORY
Turkey Tail is a thin, shelf-like bracket fungus found on fallen branches, stumps and decaying logs. Its many bands of brown, grey, cream and rust give it the look of layered feathers, which is how it earned its name. It is common, beautiful and easy to overlook until one kneels close to the wood.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
As a white-rot fungus, Turkey Tail helps break down wood and return its matter to the living system. It turns fallen timber into habitat, food and soil, opening space for insects, mosses, microbes and future growth. It is one of the quiet recyclers that keeps woodland cycles moving.

TRADITIONS & LORE
Turkey Tail has become one of the most widely recognised medicinal fungi in modern herbal and mycological conversation, while its patterned bands have long inspired simple wonder. It teaches that beauty often appears in the work of decay, and that endings can be richly inhabited.

A REFLECTION
