Field Maple
Acer campestre
The hedgerow leaf that turns the margins gold.

NATURAL HISTORY
Field Maple is Britain’s native maple, most often found in hedgerows, woodland edges and old field boundaries. It is a modest tree, yet full of grace, with small five-lobed leaves, neat branching and paired winged seeds. In autumn it turns cleat yellow and amber, lighting quiet margins with colour.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
Field Maple supports many insects and offers shelter for birds within thick hedges and mixed countryside edges. Its flowers feed pollinators in spring, and its leaves and seeds become part of the wider web of seasonal exchange. It is a tree of connected places — lanes, fields, gardens and village greens.

TRADITIONS & LORE
Though less celebrated than oak or ash, Field Maple has long belonged to the lived texture of rural life. It is a tree of boundaries, craft and everyday beauty, often woven into hedge-lines and farmed landscapes. Its gift is not grandeur but companionship — a native presence that belongs by staying near.

A REFLECTION
Small in stature and bright in turning,
Field Maple keeps the beauty of the edge.
It teaches that quiet places, faithfully held,
can glow with their own importance.
