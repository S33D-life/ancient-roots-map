Barn Owl
Tyto alba
The pale-winged hunter of dusk and meadow.

NATURAL HISTORY
Barn Owl is one of Britain’s most recognisable birds of prey, known for its pale plumage, heart-shaped face and almost silent flight. It hunts mainly at dusk and after dark, quartering meadows and rough grass for voles and other small mammals. It nests in barns, towers, tree cavities and other quiet sheltered places.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
Barn Owls depend on rich grassland habitats, hedgerows and field margins that hold abundant prey. Their pellets tell the story of local small-mammal life, making them messengers of place as well as hunters within it. Where Barn Owls thrive, a whole web of rough meadow life is often still intact.

TRADITIONS & LORE
In folklore, Barn Owls have often been seen as ghost-birds, night-watchers or omens, their pale forms drifting through the dark. Yet they also carry another meaning: stillness, precision and listening. They belong to the threshold between fear and wonder, reminding us that the night is full of presence.

A REFLECTION
Soft-winged and lantern-faced, the Barn Owl
moves through dusk with still attention.
It teaches that there is a way of knowing
that comes not by noise, but by listening.
