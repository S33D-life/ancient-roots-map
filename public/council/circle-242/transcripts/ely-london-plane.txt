Great London Plane of Ely
Platanus × acerifolia
The broad-canopied witness of open sky and stone.

NATURAL HISTORY
London plane is a vigorous hybrid tree, long valued in towns, parks and avenues. Its patchwork bark peels in plates to reveal pale green, cream and olive beneath, while broad maple-like leaves and hanging seed-balls mark the seasons. It can grow immense, casting a spacious canopy that feels both civic and sheltering.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
A mature plane offers shade, roosting places and refuge for urban wildlife. Rough bark, old cavities and branch architecture create niches for insects, lichens and birds, while fallen leaves feed the living soil below. In open landscapes it becomes a gathering tree — a living roof for meeting, rest, play and quiet thought.

TRADITIONS & LORE
Plane trees have long been planted where people gather: beside roads, in commons, near schools and along public walks. They are trees of welcome, endurance and civic memory — companions to conversation and crossing. The Great London Plane of Ely stands in that tradition, joining rooted strength with an open, generous presence.

A REFLECTION
Wide-limbed and weather-bright, the Plane
holds a room beneath the sky.
Its bark remembers change without losing form,
and its shade invites the world to draw near.
