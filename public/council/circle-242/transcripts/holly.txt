Holly
Ilex aquifolium
The evergreen that holds the winter.

NATURAL HISTORY
Holly is a native tree of Britain, found in woodlands, hedgerows and ancient landscapes. It can live for hundreds of years, reaching 10–20 metres, often as a tree or a dense, sheltering shrub. Its glossy, spiny leaves and bright red berries make it one of the most recognisable trees in our winter world.

ECOLOGY & CONNECTIONS
Holly is a vital winter resource. Its berries feed birds such as blackbird, thrush, robin and waxwing when other food is scarce. Its dense, evergreen cover offers shelter and nesting places, and its spring flowers provide nectar and pollen for bees and other pollinators. Holly helps to keep woodland edges rich and resilient.

TRADITIONS & LORE
In Celtic tradition, Holly was a tree of protection, renewal and life through winter. It was hung in homes to bring good fortune and keep away harm, and often paired with Ivy as a symbol of balance — light and dark, rest and return. Its ever-living leaves remind us that life continues, even when the land seems to sleep.
